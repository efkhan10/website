The Problem

Rising rents are making it difficult for young professionals to find affordable housing near their workplaces, and many face or experience homelessness. Post pandemic, office vacancy has surged, leaving valuable CBD real estate unused, and many city dwellers face increased loneliness.

The Solution

Converting vacant office spaces into co-living apartments provides affordable housing near workplaces and fosters community and socialization through shared spaces to combat urban isolation. This not only addresses housing affordability, but also promotes sustainability by repurposing existing structures instead of new construction.

Evidence

  1. Government Regulation Assembly Bill 1532 to fast track office-to-housing conversions 421-g NYC tax exemptions for commercial to residential conversions
  2. Supply of Buildings LA county alone: 2300 properties that could produce between 72,000 - 113,000 housing units (CBRE)
  3. Demand is Significatn Growing movement to urban centres. Rising rents costs is ubiquitous
  4. Already Underway 42 office conversions completed as of Dec 2022, 217 more planned (CBRE)

What does co-living mean?

Co-living is a housing model where indivduals have their own room but alsoshare amentities such as kitchen, living spaces, and bathrooms. It is the most practical solution when converting office spaces into apartments. Below are visual examples of how the office spaces transform into living spaces.

After reasearching office conversions, we went with a combination of example 3 and 4. Numbers show that converting an office building into private apartments can be more costly than tearing down the building and rebuilding from scratch. To continue to be mindful about the environemnt while also having a sustanable business model, we went with option 3.

Surveying Customer Interest/Profiles

Survey Questions

  1. Describe your last home search? What were some challenges you faced? Did you have to make any compromises?
  2. What specific amenities or features in your current home make it feel comfortable to live in? Were there any that were a key part of your decision to move in?
  3. How does your current living space accommodate your everyday lifestyle (e.g., work from home, family size, cooking, relaxing)? Have you had to make any adjustments?
  4. Have you ever lived in a building with shared spaces or amenities? What was it like? Can you rate the experience? Positives? Negatives?
  5. The last time you moved, did you consider community spaces or amenities when choosing your building? If so, explain.
  6. Did sustainability(eg renewable energy) factor into your most recent housing decision? Did you end up choosing a more sustainable option
  7. What was your last experience with paying rent in an urban city? Were there any challenges with affordability?
  8. What was your last utility bill like? Did you feel it was too high or fair?

Person 3 - Real Estate Developer in LA Perspective1

  1. From in an investment standpoint, it has to make financial sense and be practical. Every deal is different and has to be analyzed independently. There are different strategies for different groups of investors. Office to apartment conversions now represent 38% of 150,000 apartments in future adaptive reuse projects. These types of projects are becoming more and more feasible with regulations are coming down and the upcoming change in urban landscape.
  2. Location is very important because it influences the value of the property. The way any business makes money is asking: “how do we make the customers life easier.” Downtown’s are struggling nationwide- The amount of vacant office buildings in dense urban areas is an interesting point of conversation and a problem that needs to continue to be addressed.
  3. In a development project, what you would want is a return on investment. You would want to have rooms that are 1000 SQFT. They have to be a little nicer because all the living amenities will be shared. For the arts district project, a slam dunk would be acquisition of $180 PSF (Asking price is $250 PSF). City incentives prohibit you from collecting certain returns. If you take money from the city, that puts a ceiling on the amount you can collect. You would want a low acquisition price and development costs that bring you up to $220 PSF. Unless you get a low acquisition price that allows you to collect a profit, this type of deal is not my kind of investment.
  4. Dense urban centers have buildings that go up (instead of out) which is harder to do a conversion. There are a couple ways to easily lose your shirt, one of them being project like these where there is capital intensive work.
  5. Look at the conversions already being done and the rates they are getting on finished conversions.

Creating the Ad Funnel

  • Meta ad
  • Landing page
  • Reservation form
  • $50 deposit

Every test ran through the same funnel: someone sees an ad, lands on the reservation page, fills in the form, and puts down a deposit. Pricing was set so we could judge each click against what a tenant is worth.

  • $50Deposit price
  • $1,000Discounted price, for 3 months
  • $1,200MSRP retail price
  • $200Discount (33%)

Unit economics

  • $1,500 rent per unit per month
  • $500 profit per unit per month
  • $500 x 12 months = $6,000 per tenant per year

Breakeven ROAS

We can spend up to $6,000 to win one tenant before the ad spend stops paying for itself. Anything we pay per click or per reservation is tiny next to that number.

Insight. Ad 1 won clearly. It drew twice the clicks of Ad 2 at nearly the same cost per click, while Ad 3 cost about double per click and barely got seen. We iterated on Ad 1.

The Audience Problem

Our audience was too broad. Meta restricts targeting on housing ads, so the ads were reaching people 60 and older, which made no sense for a product built for young professionals.

First response

Run the ads the way we originally wanted to, as a housing organization, and accept the risk of being flagged under the housing rules.

Where we landed

Pick a new unrestricted audience that is narrower and more focused, and rewrite the best ad with no housing language at all, positioning it around interesting innovations so Meta would not classify it as housing.

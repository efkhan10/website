Venus is a smart mirror paired with an app that allows you to try on clothes virtually equipped with advanced technology that assists users in selecting the perfect outfit for specific events, weather conditions, and situations. The mirror employs artificial intelligence and machine learning algorithms to analyze various factors and provide personalized outfit recommendations. The mirror is paired with an app to further specify your choices.

For my Final Project, I collaborated in a team of 4 to develop product: Venus, a smart mirror that helps users select personalized outfits based on weather, events, and personal style. The project combined fashion, AI, and augmented reality to simplify and elevate the daily routine of getting dressed.

I contributed to the technology implementation, app interface design, and documentation, helping integrate AI algorithms with AR to provide real-time outfit recommendations. The mirror also tracks user preferences over time to refine its suggestions, offering a truly personalized experience. As part of the project, we developed a functional prototype, complete with a companion app that allows users to manage their wardrobe, virtually try on clothes, and receive styling insights. We also explored branding, business strategy, and marketing, envisioning partnerships with fashion retailers like Zara and influencer campaigns to introduce the product to early adopters.

The result is a future-forward fashion technology, bridging everyday usability with cutting-edge AR and AI, making style effortless and interactive for a wide audience.

Venus took seven stages to get from a blank page to a pitch. Each one started with a question, ended with something we had to change, and left behind work you can see below.

define → ideate → brand → make → build → evaluate → pitch

What in the home is actually worth solving?

We started by asking what society even counts as a household. Existing solutions ranged from smart stoves and fridges to solar panels and water purifiers, which left far too much space to solve for. We picked five segments to study and interviewed peers about the solutions they wanted.

  • Sustainability
  • Augmented Reality
  • Solar Energy
  • Product Lifespans
  • Accessibility

What changedA huge space became one problem statement.

How might we simplify and enhance the way individuals move and interact within their homes using sustainable, high tech solutions like AI and AR to support daily activities?

What we heard in one peer interview

2/10values sustainability at home
9/10values ease at home
5/10fear of tech in the home
Yesopen to an AR experience

Machine learning was welcome for the kitchen and closet, but not for safety or external household processes. The biggest thing they wanted to improve was deciding what to wear, followed by meal prepping, from grocery shopping to cooking, including calorie intake.

Which idea deserves to be built?

We mind mapped, rapidly ideated, and brain dumped, working alone and together, until we had eight ideas that fit the problem statement.

  • Recycling assistant
  • Chore gamification
  • Interior design simulator
  • AI/AR mirror
  • + 4 more

To keep our own bias out of the choice, we explained each feasible idea to peers, answered their questions about viability, and let them tell us which they would actually prefer.

What changedThe mirror won. It could dress users, plan outfits, teach styling, and connect them with brands, and it solved for the most people.

What should Venus feel like?

Moodboards pulled us in every direction: elegant, playful, neutral, sophisticated, childlike, feminine, masculine. We chose neutral, sophisticated, and elegant to signal a high quality product, with maroon as the accent against black, gray, and white.

What changedWe committed to an inclusive look that does not isolate any gender or age group.

define → ideate → brand → make → build → evaluate → pitch

What does sleek and elegant actually look like in a real object?

We reached for what we knew, wood and cardboard, and both failed us. Cardboard lost its shape and was hard to reform after a bump or a fall. Wood was rigid, and that rigidity showed through the fabric, so it never felt refined.

We were balancing cost, versatility, ease, and appearance all at once, and spent a lot of time trying to force familiar materials to work.

What changedWe stopped defaulting to familiar materials and judged each option on all four factors together. The result was an elegant maroon framed mirror.

define → ideate → brand → make → build → evaluate → pitch

If the mirror is the face, what does the digital product do?

The mirror is the physical product, but the app does the heavy lifting. It registers a user's clothing, plans and curates outfits, tracks pieces that never get worn, and doubles as a remote control for the mirror.

On the technical side, I researched how the intelligence would work: a convolutional neural network that learns which clothing fits which events and weather, then learns each person's taste from the outfits they like. An AR layer reads the camera and overlays clothes on the user's body. Camera images are deleted after every use, a privacy decision we made early.

What changedEach app feature was tied to a type of user: fast track for efficiency, swiping for fashion, cost per wear for sustainability.

Key features

  • Recommendations based on weather
  • Recommendations based on calendar
  • Favorite outfits
  • Closet inventory
  • Cost per use calculator
  • Travel packing
  • Social network
  • Venus Wrapped

Is this actually for who we think it is?

Our first persona was a high earning adult who could afford a $1500 mirror. We tested in the wild with a large survey of people with different styles and finances, plus focus group interviews that went deeper into their concerns.

We learned the market was bigger than we assumed. People as young as their late teens wanted a tool to develop their style.

What changedWe split the business model: the app as a subscription, the mirror sold separately. That let us reach younger and lower earning users without losing the high earning customer.

define → ideate → brand → make → build → evaluate → pitch

How do we turn all of this into a company someone would back?

We packaged the research, product, and business model into a pitch. The ask: $100K in exchange for 10% equity, plus introductions to fashion partners and AI and AR advisors.

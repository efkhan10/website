Scout is an augmented reality outdoor companion that enhances hiking, running, climbing, and exploration through hands-free navigation, environmental awareness, and community-driven discovery.

  • Access to personal statistic dashboard i.e. pace, distance, elevation, time

  • Access to map and location specific information

  • Access to view and share community notes through 3-D icons left on the trail

  • Opt into a social treasure hunt game to find all 10 gems randomly hidden in your reality

Existing outdoor applications often require constant interaction with handheld devices, pulling users away from their surroundings. Scout explores how augmented reality can create a more immersive and connected outdoor experience by embedding information directly into the user’s environment.


Pain Points of Current Hiking

  • Distracting Tracking pace, distance, and altitude means repeatedly pulling out a phone mid-trail
  • Confusing Physical trail markers are static and outdated, with no community layer
  • Fragmented community Sightings, warnings, and hidden spots are scattered across social media, Google Maps, and word of mouth — hard to surface at the right place and time


Scout: Enhance exploration and safety during outdoor activities (hiking, climbing, backpacking, camping)

  • Real-time hiking dashboard
  • Spatial navigation and route visualization
  • Community-created AR artifacts
  • Artifact creation and discovery system
  • Gamified treasure hunt mechanics

User Testing

What worked well:
  • Positive first impressions of the starting scene UI across all testers
  • Users engaged well with the stats page, especially those already familiar with fitness tracking apps like Strava
  • The artifact discovery concept resonated with users
  • Tutorial was effective when users engaged with it fully
Areas for improvement:
  • Menu bar and empty space in certain scenes caused confusion
  • Find and report buttons were inconsistent in functionality
  • Artifacts sometimes failed to appear/trigger, preventing users from viewing messages
  • Overall UI needs refinement to feel less static and more responsive to user movement
Feature requests:
  • More gamification, with comparisons to Pokémon Go
  • Artifacts and collectible elements should feel integrated rather than distracting
  • One non-hiker user expressed more interest in gamified elements than hiking-specific features

Examples of rapid prototyping

The first iterations included a stagnant menu that would overlay at the top of the person's viewpoint, in a neutral orange-pink color. Green & blue would clash with the outdoors.

Color Changes:
Snap Engineers after testing reccomended the color purple as it is a complementary color to yellow so would overlay well with the sun

Design Changes:
To be less interruptive, the hanging horizontal menu became vertical and veered toward the right side. The statistics are now hidden behind a button, allowing hikers to feel immersed but can intuitevly access various options.

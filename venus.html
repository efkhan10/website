Venus is a smart mirror paired with an app that allows you to try on clothes virtually equipped with advanced technology that assists users in selecting the perfect outfit for specific events, weather conditions, and situations. The mirror employs artificial intelligence and machine learning algorithms to analyze various factors and provide personalized outfit recommendations. The mirror is paired with an app to further specify your choices.

What should Venus feel like?

We started building moodboards on what our product could feel like: elegant, playful, neutral, sophisticated, childlike, feminine and masculine were all potential avenues. But we decided on a neutral, sophisticated, and elegant look to communicate our high quality product. We wanted an inclusive design that didn’t isolate genders or age groups, and also looked stylish to the average eye. Maroon was our accent color of choice, with black, gray, and white to compliment its chicness.

What does sleek and elegant actually look like in a real object?

We reached for what we knew, wood and cardboard, and both failed us. Cardboard lost its shape and was hard to reform after a bump or a fall. Wood was rigid, and that rigidity showed through the fabric, so it never felt refined. We were balancing cost, versatility, ease, and appearance all at once, and spent a lot of time trying to force familiar materials to work.

When choosing the design of our mirror, we aimed to have a mirror that would both be androgynous/gender neutral and retain a timeless/forever feel. The mirror is inherently large with a maroon border–gender neutral color. Our research in colors gave us the knowledge to pick colors that had no obvious gender association(both red and brown are cited as gender neutral). The rectangular shape of the mirror is a simple and timeless shape that can seamlessly integrate with any bedroom or bathroom interior design.

Digital product development

Our physical product was an elegant maroon framed mirror, but our digital product would do the larger grunt work. We orchestrated an application that could register user clothing, plan and curate outfits, keep track of items that weren’t used, and work hand in hand with the mirror as a remote control. It was built to handle the data the mirror would collect, and with the help of AI, learn the users style preferences and dressing patterns.

Key App Features

  • Reccomendations based on weather
  • Reccomendations based on calendar
  • Favorite Outfits
  • Closet Inventory
  • Cost Per Use Calculator
  • Travel/Packing
  • Venus Wrapped

technical architecture

Our concept requires use of AI and AR to allow the mirror to recommend outfits based on events, weather, and personal style and then project those outfits on the person standing in the mirror. The AI algorithms will be used to implement the app feature that recommends outfits based on weather, events, and personal style. Augmented reality would be used to project the clothes onto the person standing in the mirror. More features are also implemented and will all be associated with the app, which allows for updates based on feedback and new ideas.

consumer evaluation

After designing the interface of our application and first drafts of our physical product, we now had to evaluate the product and it’s usability. Our original user persona was a high-earning consumer, but by testing our product in the wild, we realized that we had a larger market than expected. Essentially, this mirror could solve for a younger base (starting in the late teens) who wanted to develop their style and could evolve with the mirror. Moreover, this business model could essentially create subsets of products (selling the app interface as a subscription based model) and the mirror separately. By leveraging value on the mirror for the larger user group, we could capture both a younger and lower earning audience as well as keep the older-high earning demographic.

We gained these insights through two sets of evaluation techniques: large surveys and focus-group interviews. Our large survey captured a wide range of people varying in style choices and financial positions. Their observations gave us a window into the varying public opinion on our concept, interface design, and possible features for the app. Additionally, by conducting many interviews that explained our product and its usability, we were able to go deeper into different opinions and personal concerns regarding our product. Essentially, during this stage is how we got to our final product you have here today.
